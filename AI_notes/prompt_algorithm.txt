1. notes/jn_notes.tex  (and its compiled version notes/jn_notes.pdf)
   The full notes. Read at least Section 1 (Secs. 1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3) and the appendix on the
   Fokker-Planck equation (generator, probability current). Eq. (41) of the PDF is the
   un-numbered-label align block in Sec. 1.2 "Units", right after "Using $B_0^*=1$, the
   equations of motion read". In dimensionless units (k_B = 1, B_0 = 1):

       dx/dt = v,
       dv/dt = -(4 pi l / M) I + (1/(2M)) L'(x) I^2,
       dI/dt = (4 pi l / L) v - (1/L) (R + L' v) I + (g/L) Gamma(t),

   with H = p^2/(2M) + Phi^2/(2L(x)), p = M v, Phi = L(x) I, and g = sqrt(2 T R(x)).
   The equivalent (x, p, Phi) form is Eq. (eom) in Sec. 1 (with l B_0 -> 4 pi l):

       xdot   =  dH/dp,
       pdot   = -dH/dx - (4 pi l) dH/dPhi,
       Phidot =  (4 pi l) dH/dp - R(x) dH/dPhi + g(x) Gamma(t),

   i.e. b = (A - K) grad H with constant antisymmetric A and K = diag(0, 0, R(x)),
   and the fluctuation-dissipation relation g^2 = 2 T R (Eq. (fdr)). Sec. 1.3 shows that the
   Gibbs density rho ~ exp(-H/T) (Eq. (gibbs)) is an exact stationary solution with the bar
   free to move, that the marginal in x is ~ sqrt(L(x)) (not normalizable for the rails
   unless the bar is confined), and lists discretization as a source of error.

2. ../circuit_simulation/paper/v1/paper_circuit.pdf, Section 5.1 "Algorithm" (pp. 18-19).
   The deterministic (R > 0, no noise) algorithm. It is a velocity-Verlet kick-drift-kick
   scheme:
       v_{i+1/2} = v_i + (dt/2M) [ -B0 l I_i + (1/2) L'(x_i) I_i^2 ]            (87)
       x_{i+1}   = x_i + v_{i+1/2} dt                                         (88)
       I_{i+1}   = [ (1 - dt k/2) I_i + dt B0 l v_{i+1/2} / L(x_m) ] / (1 + dt k/2) (89)
       v_{i+1}   = v_{i+1/2} + (dt/2M) [ -B0 l I_{i+1} + (1/2) L'(x_{i+1}) I_{i+1}^2 ] (90)
   with k = [R(x_m) + L'(x_m) v_{i+1/2}] / L(x_m), x_m = (x_i + x_{i+1})/2. The circuit
   equation, linear in I, is advanced by the trapezoidal (Crank-Nicolson) rule with v frozen
   at v_{i+1/2} and L, L', R evaluated at the midpoint of the drift. The scheme is second
   order (verified numerically: energy error ~ dt^2).

3. ../../../Simulation/langevin_simul/entropy-19-00647-v2.pdf
   M. Sachs, B. Leimkuhler, V. Danos, "Langevin Dynamics with Variable Coefficients and
   Nonconservative Forces: From Stationary States to Numerical Methods", Entropy 19, 647
   (2017). Read Section 1 (Eqs. (1)-(2)) and Section 4 "Numerical Discretization"
   (Eqs. (58)-(63) and Algorithm 1). Summary:
     - Langevin system dq/dt = M^{-1} p, dp/dt = F(q) - Gamma(q) p + Sigma(q) Wdot.
     - Split into A (L_A f = p.M^{-1} grad_q f), B (L_B f = F.grad_p f) and
       O (qdot = 0, pdot = -Gamma(q) p + Sigma(q) Wdot, an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process at
       frozen q). Noise and dissipation are kept together in O.
     - Exact O-step: p <- G_t p + S_t R, G_t = exp(-dt Gamma(q)), S_t S_t^T = C_t - G_t C_t G_t^T,
       Gamma C + C Gamma^T = Sigma Sigma^T (Lyapunov). Costly for matrices, so they
       approximate O by a DFD sub-splitting, p <- G^2 p + sqrt(dt) G Sigma R,
       G = exp(-dt Gamma/2), repeated K times with step dt/K (m-BAOAB).
     - Algorithm 1 as printed:
         1: INPUT (q,p), Gamma, Sigma, dt, K
         2: q <- q + (dt/2) p
         3: p <- p + (dt/2) F(q)
         4: G <- exp(-(dt/2K) Gamma(q))
         5: for i = 1..K: p <- G^2 p + G sqrt(dt/K) Sigma(q) R_i,  R_i ~ N(0, I)
         6: end for
         7: p <- p + (dt/2) F(q)
         8: q <- q + (dt/2) p
       Note: as printed, the sequence starts with the q-update (A) and then the kick (B),
       i.e. it reads A-B-O-B-A, although the text calls it BAOAB, and M = I is assumed.
       Comment on this discrepancy in a footnote or remark, state which ordering you adopt
       (B-A-O-A-B as in the name, consistent with Leimkuhler-Matthews), and why.


WHAT THE DERIVATION MUST CONTAIN
--------------------------------
Organize the subsection with short bold paragraph headers ({\bf ...:}) as the rest of
Sec. 1 does. Cover, in this order:

(a) Motivation. Why Euler-Maruyama (mentioned in Sec. 1.3) is not satisfactory, why a
    splitting that keeps the dissipation and the noise together is preferable, and what
    is gained relative to the deterministic scheme of the paper (Sec. 5.1), which cannot
    simply be reused once Gamma(t) is added (the trapezoidal rule is not a valid
    discretization of the noise term, and v_{i+1/2}-dependent k would mix noise into the
    drift of I).

(b) Choice of variables. Explain why the splitting should be done in the variables
    (x, p, Phi) rather than (x, v, I):
      - in (x, p, Phi) the noise is additive in Phi at fixed x, the dissipation is the
        single term -R Phi/L, and the term -L' v I/L of the I-equation (an artifact of the
        change of variables, Sec. 1.3) disappears;
      - H = p^2/2M + Phi^2/2L(x) has the form "kinetic energy with a position-dependent
        mass matrix diag(M, L(x))" for the momenta (p, Phi), with x the only position.
    Map the problem onto the Sachs et al. form (1)-(2) and state precisely where it does
    NOT fit (the antisymmetric coupling 4 pi l between p and Phi, the x-dependence of the
    "mass" L(x), Gamma and Sigma acting only on Phi). Then explain how the splitting idea
    still applies.

(c) The splitting. Decompose the generator G (appendix notation) into pieces whose
    flows can be solved EXACTLY. A natural route, to be verified and justified (not just
    asserted), is to split H = H_1 + H_2 with H_1 = p^2/2M and H_2 = Phi^2/2L(x), and to
    take the exact flow of each piece under the SAME non-canonical structure A:
      - "A" (flow of H_1 under A):  xdot = p/M, Phidot = 4 pi l p/M, pdot = 0
        -> exactly solvable (x and Phi shift linearly; interpret physically: the induced
           EMF 4 pi l v changes the flux by 4 pi l dx).
      - "B" (flow of H_2 under A):  xdot = 0, Phidot = 0,
        pdot = L'(x) Phi^2/(2 L(x)^2) - 4 pi l Phi/L(x)  -> a constant kick; check that
        in terms of I it is exactly the kick of Eqs. (87)/(90) of the paper.
      - "O" (dissipation + noise at fixed x and p):  dPhi = -(R(x)/L(x)) Phi dt + g(x) dW
        -> an exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck step.
    If you find a better or more natural decomposition (e.g. treating the linear p-Phi
    rotation exactly, or merging it with O as a 2x2 OU process), you may discuss it as an
    alternative, but give one definitive algorithm.
    For each piece, write the operator L_A, L_B, L_O, its exact flow over a time h, and
    show that each flow (i) preserves phase-space volume or the appropriate measure, and
    (ii) leaves the Gibbs density exp(-H/T) invariant, or explain which combination does.
    Use the conservative/dissipative split of the probability current already in the notes
    (J = J^c + J^d) wherever it shortens the argument.

(d) The exact O-step. Derive it from the solution of the OU equation at frozen x:
        Phi <- c Phi + sqrt( T L(x) (1 - c^2) ) xi,   c = exp(-R(x) h / L(x)),  xi ~ N(0,1),
    using g^2 = 2 T R (Eq. (fdr)). Show that it preserves the conditional Gaussian
    <Phi^2 | x> = T L(x) exactly, for any h. Compare explicitly with the O-step of the
    m-BAOAB (DFD with K substeps, Eq. (63)): show that because Gamma = R/L and Sigma = g
    are scalars here (acting on the single component Phi), the exact OU step is cheap, the
    Lyapunov equation (62) is solved trivially (C = g^2 L/(2R) = T L), and multiple time
    stepping is unnecessary (K = 1 with the exact step; the DFD update tends to the exact
    one as K -> infinity). Comment on the limit R h/L -> 0 and on L(x) -> 0 (x -> 0).

(e) The algorithm. Assemble the symmetric sequence B(h/2) A(h/2) O(h) A(h/2) B(h/2) with
    h = dt, and write it in two equivalent forms:
      1. in the variables (x, p, Phi), as the natural output of the derivation;
      2. in the variables (x, v, I) used by the code and by Eq. (41), with I = Phi/L(x)
         recomputed after each sub-step that changes x or Phi, so that it can be compared
         line by line with Eqs. (87)-(90) of the paper. State which quantities (L, L', R)
         are evaluated where, and how many evaluations per step are needed.
    Present the final algorithm as a numbered list of assignments in an
    \begin{enumerate} ... \end{enumerate} or an align environment (the preamble has no
    algorithm package; do NOT add new packages unless strictly necessary, and if you do,
    say so). Use the dimensionless units of Sec. 1.2 (B_0 = 1, coupling 4 pi l, k_B = 1,
    g = sqrt(2 T R)), but also note how to restore l B_0.

(f) Consistency checks, derived analytically:
      - Deterministic limit T = 0: the O-step reduces to Phi <- exp(-R h/L) Phi. Show
        that for R = 0 and T = 0 the scheme is a symmetric (time-reversible),
        volume-preserving, second-order integrator of the Hamiltonian dynamics, and relate
        it to the velocity-Verlet scheme of the paper (state the differences: e.g. exact
        flux update in the drift vs Crank-Nicolson in I).
      - Weak order: state the expected weak order (2) for general observables, and what is
        known (from Leimkuhler-Matthews / Sachs et al.) about the high accuracy of BAOAB
        for configurational averages; be honest about which statements are proved for the
        standard case and which are expected here because of the x-dependent coefficients
        and the non-canonical coupling.
      - Equilibrium: at which level the algorithm reproduces <M v^2 | x> = <L I^2 | x> = T
        (Sec. 1.3 "Check in the simulation"), and the remaining O(dt^p) bias.
      - Fixed bar (x frozen, p ignored): the scheme reduces to the exact update of the
        Johnson-noise OU process of Sec. 1.1.

(g) Practical remarks: choice of dt relative to the time scales L/R and 1/omega
    (Eq. (oscillator)); the x -> 0 region where L -> 0; confining the bar (wall/potential)
    so that a global equilibrium exists, and how a confining force enters the B-step; the
    random numbers needed per step (one Gaussian); and suggested numerical tests (energy
    error vs dt at T = 0, R = 0; histograms of v and I in x-bins at T > 0; convergence of
    <M v^2> and <L I^2> vs dt, compared with Euler-Maruyama).
